02 THE PROGRAMME · PAGE 02 / 05

Where the cover runs thin

Flood at Elmwood is a $1M NFIP primary with no business interruption, under about $4M of excess that does carry it. Wind carries BI too, but sits under a percentage deductible. Four gaps came up on the call. Three were MMA's. One is ours.

SELECT A GAP

GAP A · RAISED BY MMASOURCEDP09

Business interruption over the NFIP primary

MMA would welcome BI and extra expense on the NFIP layer. NFIP cannot write BI, so this is a gap in the federal product, not an incumbent pricing position.

WE WOULD NEED
A difference-in-conditions or buffer layer over the $1M NFIP primary, carrying BI and extra expense.

WHY IT MATTERS
If the 3.1 ft floor height is measured from grade, this is the whole flood case (page 03).

GAP B · RAISED BY MMASOURCEDP10

More excess, or a better price on it

MMA would take more cover on the excess at a better price. It sees no need for further excess at Palmetto.

WE WOULD NEED
Capacity above the current $5M of total flood limits, or a better rate on the existing $4M excess.

STILL MISSING
Exclusions, sublimits and excess layer terms, so a form complements the programme rather than overlapping it.

GAP C · OUR PROPOSAL, NOT AN MMA REQUESTUNVERIFIEDP11

A lower percentage wind deductible

MMA described the percentage wind deductible and the market's move from 5% to 3%, 2% and sometimes 1%. It did not ask us to buy it down. We test it with MMA before treating it as a requirement.

WE WOULD NEED
A percentage deductible buy-down. It raises the severity we retain, in the least validated part of our model (page 04).

STILL MISSING
The percentage itself, and whether it applies to named storms or to all wind.

GAP D · RAISED BY MMASOURCEDP12

Blanket excess flood across both sites

A single excess flood form across locations, carrying BI and dropping down as primary where NFIP does not respond.

WE WOULD NEED
A multi-location form. That is a question of form at least as much as of price.

ACCUMULATION
MMA judged the two sites a big enough spread. No correlated event set exists yet, so this is a judgement, not a result.

We write surplus lines only: flood, wind, and hail where relevant. Primary property and all-risk are out of scope.P13

ANNEX A · METHOD

How the two engines work

Flood interventions change the hazard: less water reaches the building. Wind interventions change the building: the same wind does less damage. Each engine runs the same site twice and takes the difference.M01M03

FLOOD · BASELINE AGAINST BASIN

  1. 1Footprints

    Building outlines on the title, one polygon per building.

    MODELLED
  2. 2Depth grids

    Hydraulic model grids for each return period, 1-in-10 to 1-in-500. Durations of 1 to 24 hours collapse to a maximum.

    MODELLED
  3. 3Depth join

    Depth sampled under each building and kept per building, never averaged across the site.

    MODELLED
  4. 4Damage curves

    US depth-damage shape times dollars per sq ft. The floor offset defaults to zero, slab on grade: the assumption at issue.

    ASSUMEDF09
  5. 5EAL

    Loss annualised across the return-period ladder. Baseline minus basin is the avoided loss.

    MODELLED
  6. 6BI, not yet

    A duration schedule is drafted, on illustrative figures. It is not in the results.

    ILLUSTRATIVEM02

WIND · AS IT STANDS AGAINST HARDENED

  1. 1Hazard

    An illustrative windspeed and return-period ladder. Not yet a site lookup.

    ILLUSTRATIVEW12
  2. 2Vulnerability

    Hazus 6.1 wind damage functions, chosen by the building's attributes.

    ASSUMEDW11
  3. 3Financial

    Structure, contents and business interruption, in dollars.

    ILLUSTRATIVE
  4. 4Difference

    The same hazard through the building as it stands and as hardened. The gap is the avoided loss.
